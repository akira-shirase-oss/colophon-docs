Saving never changes what readers get. Publishing does, and in Colophon it always goes through a
**publish request**: a list of exact versions of entries that someone approves, and that become the
next **release** when published. This page is for writers, reviewers and whoever publishes.

## Publish, or Ask for review

What the editor's side panel offers depends on your role:

- **Publish** (or **Publish changes** for an entry that is **Live with changes**) when your role may
  approve and publish, like the built-in **Owner** and **Publisher**. One press makes a publish request for the
  saved version, approves it as your own recorded review and publishes it: *Goes live for readers at
  once, as the next release. Your approval is recorded.*
- **Ask for review** when your role may edit but not publish, like **Editor** and **Developer**: *Someone
  who can publish approves it and puts it live.*

The button waits for your changes to be saved (*Saving first…*): only a saved version can be
published. On a **Live** entry with nothing new to publish there is no publish button; it comes back
as **Publish changes** as soon as you save a change, including a change made right after publishing.

![Mina's view of a new entry: Draft, Saved just now, the Ask for review button, and "Someone who can publish approves it and puts it live."](/screens/studio/ask-for-review.webp)

After **Ask for review**, the panel says *Sent for review.* with **See the publish request**. After
**Publish**, it says *Live as Release 4. Its receipt shows what reached your website.*

If the project has [review stages](#review-stages) and one of them needs someone other than the
sender, **Publish** stops after your own approvals and says which stage still waits: *Not published:
It still needs approval at Fact check — someone other than whoever sent it for review has to decide
it.* The request is kept; **Open the publish request** takes you to it.

To publish several entries together, tick them in the content type's list and press **Publish 3** (or
**Ask for review of 3**), or gather them on the **Publish requests** page.

## Publish requests

Open **Publish requests** in the sidebar, under **Publishing**. The number beside it counts requests
waiting for review.

![The Publish requests page: Rhubarb fool, Changes asked for; Plum crumble, Lemon tart and Lemon curd, Published; each with its number of entries and when it was updated](/screens/studio/publish-requests.webp)

**New publish request** gathers several entries into one: give it a **Title**, tick the entries to
publish (each publishes its current version), tick **Unpublish** beside live entries to take them down,
and press **Create publish request**. *Later edits don't change it until you update it.*

A request is in one of these states:

| State | Means |
|---|---|
| **Not sent for review** | Made, not yet sent. Someone who can edit its entries presses **Ask for review**. |
| **In review** | Sent; a reviewer decides. |
| **Changes asked for** | A reviewer pressed **Request changes**. |
| **Edited since: update it** | An entry in it was saved again after it was made. |
| **Approved** | Every approval it needs is in, on exactly these versions. |
| **Approval expired** | An approval lasts seven days. It needs approving again. |
| **Published** | It went live as a release. |

## Review a publish request

For reviewers: the built-in **Publisher** and **Owner**, or any role that may approve.

<Steps>

1. Open the request. The bar under its title shows the progress: **Prepared**, **Sent for review**,
   **Approved**, **Live**.

2. Read **Entries**. Each is marked **New**, **Updated** or **Taken down**, and **Changes** shows what
   readers would get compared with what they have now. **Preview on your site** shows the whole
   request on your website: see [Preview](/studio/preview/#preview-a-publish-request).

3. In **Review**, choose which stage you are **Deciding** if the project has stages, and write a
   **Comment (optional)**.

4. Press **Approve this version**, or **Request changes**.

</Steps>

![Kai reviewing Plum crumble: In review, the progress bar at Sent for review, the entry's changes, the Review stages Editorial and Fact check both Waiting, and the Review panel with Deciding, a comment, Approve this version and Request changes](/screens/studio/publish-request.webp)

*You approve exactly the version on this page. If it changes, your approval no longer counts.* When
someone saves an entry in the request again, the request shows **Edited since: update it** and the
entry **edited since it was added**. Someone who can edit presses **Update to the latest versions**,
and the request needs reviewing again.

**History**, on the right, records every step: who approved or asked for changes, at which stage,
with their comment; *on an earlier version* marks a review of versions the request no longer holds.

## Publish an approved request

Once the request is **Approved**, someone whose role may publish presses **Publish**. The request
becomes the next release and the progress bar reaches **Live**.

![Plum crumble after publishing: Published as release 3, every progress step done, both review stages Approved by Kai Tan, and the History of approvals](/screens/studio/request-published.webp)

If another release went live while you were looking, publishing stops instead of skipping it: *Another
release was published first. Check what is live now, then publish again.* An approved request can also
be [scheduled](/studio/scheduling/) for a later time.

## Review stages

By default one approval publishes a request. **Review stages** make publishing need a named sign-off
at each stage, for example *Editorial* and *Fact check*. Owners and developers set them up under
**Settings** → **Review stages**: see [Settings](/studio/settings/#review-stages).

- Every stage must approve the request's current versions, in any order. The list's order is for
  people reading it.
- **Request changes** at any stage holds the whole request until a newer approval at that stage.
- A stage marked **Someone other than the sender must approve** never counts an approval from whoever
  sent the request for review.

A request with stages lists them under **Review stages**, each **Waiting**, **Approved**, **Changes
asked for** or **Approval expired**, and who decided it.

## Releases and receipts

Every publish makes a numbered release, and release numbers only go up. Open **Releases** in the
sidebar to see them, newest first: each with its title, how many entries it changed or took down, who
published it and when, and its label. The card at the bottom of the sidebar, **Live now**, names the
release that is live now.

Press a release's label for its **receipt**: what happened after it went live. (Its title opens the
publish request it came from.)

![The receipt of Release 4, Apple cake and Pear tart: published by Akira Shirase, 2 entries changed, the release slider, the Published badge, and empty Website connections and site checks](/screens/studio/rollback-receipt.webp)

The receipt shows who published it and when, the entries it changed, a slider to step to **Older** and
**Newer** releases, and one label for how far the release got:

| Label | Means |
|---|---|
| **Published** | It is live in Colophon, and anything that reads Colophon has it. Nothing has confirmed your website yet. |
| **Website update pending** | A [website connection](/api/website-connections/) is still delivering it. The label settles by itself. |
| **Verified at 2 locations** | A site check read this release, or a later one, on your live site, from that many places. |
| **Needs attention** | A connection gave up. **What needs attention** says which, and what to do next. |

Under the label, **Website connections** lists what each connection did with this release, and **What
the site checks saw** lists every look a site check took. **The publish request** links back to the
request it came from, and **Roll back to this release** is on every release but the latest: see
[Rollback](/studio/rollback/).

## Take an entry down

On a live entry, press **Unpublish** (or **Ask to unpublish** when your role can't publish). It is a
publish request too, whose one item takes the entry down; with **Unpublish** it goes through at once:
*Taken down in Release 9. Readers no longer get it; publish it again to put it back.*

A taken-down entry is gone from every release, past ones included: the delivery API answers `410 Gone`
for it (see [Taken-down content](/api/delivery/#taken-down-content)). It stays in the Studio as a
**Draft**, with its history, and publishing it again puts it back. Colophon refuses to take down an
entry that a published entry still links to: see [Linked entries](/studio/linked-entries/).
