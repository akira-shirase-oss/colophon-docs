Preview shows your unpublished draft rendered by your real website, so you see it with the site's
own layout and styles before readers do. Nobody else can open it: each preview is a private link that
ends after a short while.

It has two halves. An owner or developer tells Colophon the site's preview address once. Then every
writer uses **Preview** on an entry, or **Preview on your site** on a publish request. The site needs a
small preview route, which your developer adds with the SDK.

## Set the preview address

For owners and developers (the roles that manage website connections).

<Steps>

1. Open **Settings** → **Website connections**. Under **Preview on your site**, find **Preview
   address**.

2. Type your site's preview route, with placeholders Colophon fills in for each entry:

   | Placeholder | Becomes |
   |---|---|
   | `{code}` | *Required.* A one-time code your route trades for access to the draft. |
   | `{collection}` | The entry's content type, like `recipes`. |
   | `{id}` | The entry's id. |
   | `{slug}` | Its URL name, when it has one. |
   | `{locale}` | Its language, like `en`. |

   For example: `https://recipes.example.com/api/preview?code={code}&path=/recipes/{id}`.

3. Press **Save**. The card says **On** and *Previews open on* your site's address.

</Steps>

![Preview on your site, On: the Preview address with the code and id placeholders, what each placeholder becomes, and the Save and Turn off buttons](/screens/studio/preview-address.webp)

Use an `https://` address. Plain `http://` works only for `localhost` on a development server. The
address is at most 2,048 characters. **Turn off** removes it, and Preview is then unavailable until
someone sets it again. Other members see where previews open, but can't change it.

## Preview an entry

<Steps>

1. Save your changes. Preview always shows the last **saved** version, never what is only on your
   screen.

2. In the entry editor, press **Preview** above the fields and choose:

   - **Side by side**: *your site beside the fields*. On a narrow screen this is **In this page**,
     which switches between editing and the preview.
   - **Open on my site**: *in a new tab, as your site shows it*.

   ![The Preview menu open on an entry: Side by side, Your site beside the fields; Open on my site, In a new tab, as your site shows it](/screens/studio/preview-menu.webp)

3. Keep writing. After each save, the Studio tells the page in the pane, and the page reloads with
   your change.

</Steps>

The pane has its own toolbar: the preview width (**Phone** 360 pixels, **Tablet** 768, **Desktop**
1280, or **Fit** the pane), **Reload the preview**, **Open in a new tab** and **Close the preview**. A
line over the page says what it shows:

| It says | Means |
|---|---|
| **Live preview** | Your site confirmed it is showing your draft. |
| **Shows your saved draft**, then **Updated** | The page has your last save. |
| **Preview is behind: your latest changes aren't saved** | You typed after the last save. It catches up when the save goes. |
| **Not confirmed** | The page loaded, but didn't say it is showing a draft. |
| **Not showing your draft** | The page said it isn't in preview mode. |

If Preview is greyed out, the editor says why: *Preview on your site isn't set up.* An owner or
developer sees **Set it up**, which opens Website connections.

## Preview a publish request

A publish request can be previewed as a whole: every entry in it, at exactly the versions it holds,
together.

<Steps>

1. Open the request from **Publish requests**.

2. Press **Preview on your site**. It opens in a new tab.

</Steps>

*Shows exactly what this request will publish, for 15 minutes. If the request changes, preview it
again.* Once the request is published, the button goes: the site shows it to everyone.

## How long a preview lasts

Each preview is a private link made for you. The address the Studio opens carries a one-time code
that works once, within 60 seconds; your site trades it for access that lasts 15 minutes. An entry
preview is renewed by itself while it stays open in the Studio, up to 8 hours, and ends when you close
it. That is why a preview link can't be passed around: whoever opens it later sees *This preview
ended*, and **Start again** makes a new one.

## When it doesn't show your draft

| The pane says | What to do |
|---|---|
| *We can't tell whether your site loaded the preview* | The page didn't confirm within 10 seconds. Press **Open in a new tab**, or **Check the address**. If the site's preview route isn't built yet, that is the developer's step below. |
| *Your browser kept the preview cookie out of this frame* | Your browser blocks third-party cookies inside the pane, so the site shows the published page. Press **Open in a new tab**, where it always works. |
| *This preview ended* | It ran out, or was closed. Press **Start again**. |
| *Preview on your site isn't set up* | An owner or developer sets the [preview address](#set-the-preview-address). |

## What your website must do

Colophon never renders your site; it opens your site's preview route with a one-time code. The
developer adds that route once:

1. **A preview route** at the address above. It trades the code for a short-lived preview token, keeps
   the token in a cookie of its own, and redirects to the page.
2. **Pages that read drafts** with that token, from Colophon's preview API, instead of the published
   content.
3. **Headers that keep previews private**: never cached, never indexed, and framed only by your
   Studio.
4. **A small script** on preview pages that tells the Studio the page is ready and reloads it after
   each save.

The TypeScript SDK provides all four for any framework: see
[Draft preview on your own site](/sdk/#draft-preview-on-your-own-site).
