A schedule publishes an approved publish request later, at a time you choose, exactly as if you
pressed **Publish** then. This page is for whoever publishes: scheduling needs the same permission as
publishing.

## Schedule a publish request

<Steps>

1. Get the request approved. Until then its **Schedule** panel says *Once it's approved, it can be
   scheduled to publish at a time you choose.* See [Publishing and review](/studio/publishing/).

2. Open the request. Under **Schedule** (*Publish this exact version later, at a time you choose.*),
   set the **Date**, the **Time** and the **Time zone**. The panel suggests tomorrow at 09:00 in your
   own time zone, marked **(yours)**.

   ![The Schedule panel: the Date set to tomorrow, Time 09:00, Time zone Europe/London, and the Schedule button](/screens/studio/schedule-form.webp)

3. Press **Schedule**.

</Steps>

The panel now says when it publishes, in the time zone you chose and, if that isn't yours, where you
are:

![The Schedule panel once scheduled: Publishes tomorrow at 09:00:00 GMT+0 (Europe/London), the same moment in UTC where you are, Scheduled by Akira Shirase, just now, and the Cancel the schedule button](/screens/studio/schedule-pending.webp)

The **Releases** page lists every pending schedule, and the last ones that ran, under **Scheduled**.

### Why a time zone

A schedule keeps the local time and the named time zone you chose (`Europe/London`, not an offset like
`+01:00`). 09:00 in London stays 09:00 in London whether the clocks are on summer time or not, and
your colleagues see the moment in their own time too.

## When the clocks change

Twice a year, many time zones have a time that happens twice (the clocks go back) or not at all (they
go forward). Colophon never guesses which moment you meant. If the time you chose is one of those,
**Schedule** asks:

![The clocks go back that night in Europe/London, so 01:30 happens twice. Which one? Two choices, Sunday 31 October 2027 at 01:30:00 BST and at 01:30:00 GMT+0, and the Schedule at this moment button](/screens/studio/schedule-choice.webp)

- *The clocks go back that night in …, so 01:30 happens twice. Which one?* Pick the first or the
  second 01:30.
- *The clocks go forward that night in …, so 01:30 doesn't happen. Publish at:* pick the moment just
  before or just after the gap.

Then press **Schedule at this moment**. Changing the time zone clears the question.

## The limits

- The time must be in the future, and at most a year and a day ahead.
- It must be before the approval expires. An approval lasts seven days, so a request approved today can
  be scheduled up to seven days from the approval; for a later date, have it approved closer to the
  time. Otherwise: *The approval expires before that time; schedule it earlier, or have it approved
  closer to the time.*
- A request has one pending schedule at a time: *It is already scheduled. Cancel that schedule to
  choose another time.*

## When it runs

At the chosen moment Colophon publishes the request as the person who scheduled it, with every check a
publish makes, on top of whatever is live then. The schedule's history in the panel then says how it
went:

| It says | Means |
|---|---|
| *Published on time, as release 12* | Done. |
| *Published late, as release 12*, marked **delayed** | It started more than a minute after the chosen time, for example because Colophon was stopped then. It still published. |
| *Not published: …* | It was **blocked**, and published nothing. The reason follows. |

The **Scheduled** list on **Releases** says the same with a status badge: **Scheduled**, **Published ·
Release 12**, **Published late · Release 12**, **Not published** (with the reason under it) or
**Cancelled**.

A schedule is blocked, never half-done, when publishing would be refused at that moment: an entry in
the request was saved again, the approval expired, the person who scheduled it can no longer publish
(their role changed or they left the project), or a value another published entry holds, such as a URL
name, is in the way. Fix the cause, get the request approved again, and schedule it again.

## Cancel a schedule

Press **Cancel the schedule**. The person who scheduled it, or anyone who may publish the request, can
cancel it; anyone else sees *Its author or someone who can publish it can cancel it.* Cancelling never
undoes a release: a schedule that already published stays published.
